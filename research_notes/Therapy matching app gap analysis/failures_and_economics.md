# Failures, Controversies and Business Economics in Digital Mental Health (as of November 3, 2026)

Scope note: This covers the US unless a non-US example is labeled. All dates are given explicitly. The web-search budget ran out before locum-tenens economics and a few closures could be verified. Those items are listed under Gaps and are not asserted.

## 1. Notable failures and scandals, and why each happened

### Takeaway
The large failures fall into three groups:
- **Growth-at-all-costs DTC prescribing that turned criminal or regulatory:** Cerebral and Done.
- **Business models that never found a payer:** Pear, Woebot's consumer app and Mindstrong.
- **DTC consumer therapy shrinking while payer-funded therapy grows:** BetterHelp and Talkspace.

By 2026 the two best-known DTC therapy brands had both turned toward insurance. Talkspace was sold to a hospital operator for about 40% less than its SPAC valuation.

### Cited Findings
**Mindstrong (shut down 2023)**
- Founded 2014 (some sources say 2017). It raised about $160M, including a $100M Series C in 2020, and used smartphone "biomarkers" to detect mental health conditions. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/mindstrong-sells-tech-assets-sondermind-shuts-down-operations); [OPEN MINDS](https://openminds.com/market-intelligence/bulletins/sondermind-acquires-mindstrongs-technology-mindstrong-closes-down/)
- January 2023: it announced the end of patient care services. Care stopped on March 10, 2023, 128 positions were cut under a WARN notice, and the Menlo Park HQ closed. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/mental-health-platform-mindstrong-close-hq-lay-128-employees)
- March 2023: SonderMind bought Mindstrong's technology (EHR, clinical notes, care planning, ML assets) and about 20 staff for undisclosed terms. Mindstrong then ended all operations. — [Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2023/03/22/sondermind-acquires-mindstrongs-tech-as-part-of-final-wind-down/)
- No single official reason was given. Coverage ties the collapse to the steep 2022–23 fall in digital health funding. — [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/sondermind-acquires-mindstrong-technology-digital-mental-health/645794/)

**Cerebral (FTC/DOJ privacy case, 2024; DOJ controlled-substance case, 2024)**
- April 2024: the FTC and DOJ filed a proposed order alleging that Cerebral shared sensitive data on about 3.2 million consumers with platforms including LinkedIn, Snapchat and TikTok through tracking tools, without consent. The order also targets hard-to-cancel subscriptions.
  - It permanently bans Cerebral from using or disclosing health data for advertising.
  - It requires a privacy and security program, a simple cancellation method and deletion of most consumer data. — [HIT Consultant (Jun 11, 2024)](https://hitconsultant.net/2024/06/11/cerebral-settles-ftc-charges-over-data-privacy-and-deceptive-practices); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/cerebral-agrees-7m-settlement-ftc-doj-over-user-data-sharing-cancellation-practices)
- Reported dollar amounts conflict. Fierce and HealthExec report about $7M. Becker's reports a "$15M settlement", reflecting a larger civil penalty that was largely suspended because Cerebral could not pay. — [HealthExec](https://healthexec.com/topics/healthcare-management/healthcare-policy/mental-health-platform-fined-7m-ftc-sharing-data-advertisers); [Becker's Behavioral Health](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/?p=2531)
- Former CEO Kyle Robertson did not settle. The FTC said his charges "will be decided by the court." I found no ruling. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/cerebral-agrees-7m-settlement-ftc-doj-over-user-data-sharing-cancellation-practices)
- May 2025: more than 40,000 consumers were reported as due compensation. — [MyChesCo](https://www.mychesco.com/a/news/national/ftc-reaches-settlement-with-cerebral-inc-resulting-in-refunds-for-thousands-of-consumers/)
- November 2024: Cerebral entered a non-prosecution agreement with the US Attorney for the Eastern District of New York (EDNY) over efforts to "increase prescriptions of Adderall and other controlled substances."
  - It forfeited $3,652,000. A $2,922,000 fine was deferred for inability to pay, over a 30-month term.
  - From October 2020 Cerebral prescribed controlled substances. From at least October 2021 it tracked the share of controlled-substance prescriptions going to ADHD patients without comorbidities.
  - Remediation from May 2022 included terminating the CEO. — [DOJ EDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/telehealth-company-cerebral-agrees-pay-over-36-million-connection-business-practices); [Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2024/11/05/cerebral-hit-with-6-6m-penalty-in-settlement-with-prosecutors/)

**Done Global (indicted 2024, convicted 2025, sentenced 2026)**
- Founded 2019 by Ruthia He, a former Facebook product designer with no medical background. Done ran a subscription ADHD service built on 30-minute video evaluations.
  - Clinicians were paid for processing refills rather than for follow-up visits, and some approved renewals in under a minute. — [Wikipedia (citing DOJ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Done_Global)
- Indicted June 13, 2024. Prosecutors alleged more than 40 million stimulant pills were prescribed, generating over $100M in revenue. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/digital-health-company-done-indicted-alleged-100m-illegal-adderall-distribution-scheme)
- November 18, 2025: a federal jury convicted He and clinical president David Brody of distributing Adderall over the internet and conspiring to commit health care fraud. He was also convicted of obstruction. — [HHS-OIG](https://oig.hhs.gov/fraud/enforcement/founderceo-and-clinical-president-of-digital-health-company-convicted-in-100m-adderall-distribution-and-health-care-fraud-scheme); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/founder-telehealth-startup-done-convicted-adderall-fraud-scheme)
- July 2026: He was sentenced to 72 months and a $1M fine, and Brody to 24 months and a $1M fine. — [Wikipedia (citing July 7, 2026 DOJ release)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Done_Global); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/founder-telehealth-startup-done-sentenced-six-years-prison-adderall-fraud-scheme)
- The DOJ sentencing release title cites "37 million pills" and a "$90 Million" scheme, which differs from the indictment-era figures of 40M pills and $100M. — [AFS Law summary](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/investigations-blog/done-global-founder-and-clinical-president-sentenced-90-million)

**Pear Therapeutics (Chapter 11, April 2023)**
- Pear filed for Chapter 11 and laid off nearly all staff. 2022 revenue was $12.7M against a $75.5M net loss. — [HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/articles/dtx-trailblazer-pear-therapeutics-files-for-bankruptcy-2023-04-14); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/health-tech-deals/2023/04/10/after-blazing-a-trail-in-digital-therapeutics-pear-goes-bankrupt)
- The root cause was reimbursement:
  - About half of prescriptions were covered.
  - Medicare does not cover prescription digital therapeutics (PDTs).
  - As of October 2022 only about 10 states, a few BCBS plans and SelectHealth covered any Pear product. — [AIS Health](https://www.mmitnetwork.com/aishealth/health-plan-weekly/pear-bankruptcy-filing-highlights-reimbursement-barriers-for-digital-therapeutics/); [Managed Healthcare Executive](https://www.managedhealthcareexecutive.com/view/what-does-pear-therapeutics-bankruptcy-mean-for-pdts-)
- Only about half of prescriptions were filled, and Pear collected payment on only 41% of those (Forbes figure via Telecare Aware). — [Telecare Aware](https://telecareaware.com/tag/sec/)
- Assets were auctioned in May 2023 for about $6M total. — [Pharmaceutical Technology](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/?p=244524)

### Inferences
- **Every DTC brand that survived went to payers.** Talkspace's consumer revenue fell to about 7.6% of 2025 revenue (17.5/228.9), and BetterHelp is pivoting to insurance. The market is signalling that a cash-pay therapy relationship with no institutional payer has weak durability.
- **Talkspace's exit value fell sharply.** It sold for an enterprise value of about $835M, against a SPAC valuation of about $1.4B. Even the "winner" in DTC therapy was worth less than at listing five years later, and only after becoming mostly payer-funded.
- **Revenue per BetterHelp paying user is about $205/month** in Q3 2026 ($206.6M ÷ 336K ÷ 3; my calculation). With that recurring revenue and roughly $1B in annual segment revenue, BetterHelp still runs a 2–4% adjusted EBITDA margin. A one-off model that earns one session's fee per acquired client faces far worse acquisition-cost math.
- **The criminal and regulatory cases (Cerebral, Done) share one design flaw.** Growth metrics and clinician pay were tied to transaction volume (prescriptions, refills), which overrode clinical judgment. A per-session, fill-the-slot marketplace carries the same structural temptation.

- I could not verify a reported Youper shutdown on September 30, 2026 (one aggregator claimed it, while its App Store listing showed an update on March 30, 2026). I also could not verify Sanvello's June 2024 app-store removal. Both are excluded.
- I found no ruling on the FTC's case against Cerebral's former CEO, and no current figures on Cerebral's operations or headcount.
- Mindstrong's own explanation for its failure isn't public. The funding downturn is the press's explanation, not the company's.

## 2. Prior "Tinder for therapy," swipe-based or quick-match therapist apps

### Takeaway
I found no swipe-style therapist app with verifiable US traction. The examples are either tiny App Store listings with no press coverage or reach (MindSwipe, Cocoon), or questionnaire-matching startups that stayed small or closed (Mental Health Match, Therapick, Sophia). The therapist matchers that scaled (BetterHelp, Headway, Alma, Grow, Rula) won on payment (subscription or insurance) and supply (credentialing and billing), not on the matching interface.

### Cited Findings
- **MindSwipe – Wellness Matching (iOS):** markets swiping "as simple and intuitive as swiping on dating apps," with real-time chat and a daily match limit. It was last updated December 2025. I found no press coverage or usage data. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mindswipe-wellness-matching/id6751424177); [chrome-stats listing](https://chrome-stats.com/d/id6751424177)
- **"Cocoon: Find Your Therapist" (iOS):** promises a licensed therapist "in just a swipe" and showed a 5.0 rating from only 3 reviews. A separate 2026 "Cocoon" swipe-therapy concept appears as a design portfolio project, not a launched product. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/cocoon-v2/id6479022643); [Contra portfolio](https://contra.com/p/ZPWQYoR6-cocoon-therapy-app-uiux-design)
- **Mental Health Match:**
  - Founded in Houston (2019) by Ryan Schwartz, inspired by watching a friend use a dating app while he struggled to find a therapist. It used a questionnaire that returns a top-five list, not swiping. — [InnovationMap](https://houston.innovationmap.com/houston-entrepreneur-launches-an-app-that-matches-patients-to-their-ideal-therapist-2638702851.html); [CultureMap Houston](https://houston.culturemap.com/news/innovation/06-10-19-mental-health-match-find-therapist-houston-app/)
  - By 2021 it had moved to Portland, with 2,000 therapists on the platform but only 119 total paying customers, and $1M seed raised. — [GeekWire (2021)](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/mental-health-match-helps-people-find-right-therapist-finds-new-home-portland/)
- **Therapick (2012):** an online therapist-profile service whose founder said "People have compared it to online dating." I found no evidence it lasted. — [Bloomberg (Mar 22, 2012)](https://brp-prod-bcc.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2012-03-22/therapick-online-profiles-of-therapists)
- **"Therapeasy" (2020):** billed as an "eHarmony for therapists" with questionnaire matching. Its status is unknown. — [Global Dating Insights startup roundup (May 2020)](https://www.globaldatinginsights.com/startup-roundup/the-startup-roundup-8th-may-2020/)
- **Sophia:** a counselor-matching startup that closed in November 2018. — [Coverager](https://coverager.com/sophia-has-closed-meet-headway/)
- **Frame (Los Angeles, 2020):** launched a therapist-matching app plus practice back-office tools. Its current status is unverified. — [TechCrunch (Apr 14, 2020)](https://techcrunch.com/2020/04/14/los-angeles-based-frame-launches-mental-health-gateway-for-a-pandemic-stricken-generation)
- **UX evidence:** a design case study prototyping Tinder-style therapist search found that the swipe gesture is not intuitive for people who haven't used dating apps, and warned against relying on it. — [Medium case study](https://medium.com/@jordanleslie/the-dating-app-to-help-you-find-your-next-therapist-a79502c35a19)
- **Ethics framing:** professional-ethics literature treats therapists seeing or swiping on clients on dating apps as a boundary concern, so the "Tinder" association carries clinical-boundary baggage. — [Springer, Contemporary Family Therapy (2020)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10591-020-09561-7)
- **Swipe fatigue at Tinder itself:** in 2025 Tinder moved toward AI recommendations ("Chemistry") to reduce "swipe fatigue." — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3089496)
- **Non-US marketplace failures:** Therapify (Poland, closed January 2023) and Tokitus (Lithuania, closed 2024) both failed to reach volume. — [Behavioral Healthcare Network](https://www.bhnet.org/78919/10-mental-health-startups-that-died-from-poor-financial-health/)

### Inferences
- Matching UX has never been the bottleneck. The bottlenecks are supply (therapists with open slots who take the client's payment method and are licensed in the client's state) and payment. A swipe interface on top of thin supply makes the scarcity more visible.
- Mental Health Match had 2,000 listed therapists but 119 paying customers after about two years. This suggests a directory or matching layer monetized from therapists has low willingness to pay, consistent with the failed European marketplaces.
- The "Tinder for therapy" label is likely a liability with clinicians, payers and regulators. It implies casual or superficial selection, and it collides with dating-app boundary ethics. It is fine as an internal pitch shorthand, not as a brand.

### Gaps
- I couldn't find download, revenue or funding data for MindSwipe, Cocoon, or a "Serene" swipe app that appeared in one search result without a verifiable source.
- I couldn't confirm the current status of Zencare, Frame, Kip or Mental Health Match.
- I found no US press coverage of any venture-backed company explicitly launched as "Tinder for therapy."

## 3. On-demand, drop-in or single-session therapy products

### Takeaway
"On-demand" mental health scaled only where three conditions held: the on-demand layer was coaching or text rather than licensed therapy, an employer or health plan paid, and licensed therapy was scheduled behind it (Ginger and Headspace Care). Telehealth generalists offer next-day, not same-day, psychology. The clinical evidence for single sessions is real: one session is the modal number attended, and single-session interventions have effect in trials. However, I found no verified US company that built a durable business on one-off licensed therapy sessions.

### Cited Findings
- **Ginger (now Headspace Care):**
  - It offered 24/7 text-based behavioral health coaching, with therapy and psychiatry by video, sold through employers and health plans. Its Series C was framed as "on-demand behavioral healthcare through employers and health plans." — [InsuranceNewsNet (Ginger Series C)](https://insurancenewsnet.com/oarticle/ginger-announces-35-million-in-series-c-funding-to-accelerate-global-access-to-on-demand-behavioral-healthcare-through-employers-and-health-plans)
  - Early on (2015-era), about 8% of users needed clinician care, which was guaranteed within 72 hours. That is dated and may not reflect current practice. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1877073)
  - Kaiser Permanente offers its emotional-support coaches 24/7 "with no appointment" to eligible members, free at point of use. — [Kaiser Permanente](https://business-dev2.kaiserpermanente.org/business/broker/hawaii/news-and-announcements/2023/ginger-now-headspace-care)
- **Doctor On Demand:** offers same-day physician visits but next-day psychologist and psychiatrist appointments. — [ChoosingTherapy review (2026)](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/doctor-on-demand-review/)
- **Mindful Care:** advertises same-day and next-day mental health appointments in a clinic or urgent-care style. — [Mindful Care](https://www.mindful.care/)
- **Pay-per-session options exist:**
  - Grow Therapy charges per session with no subscription. — [ChoosingTherapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/?p=105042)
  - Open Path Collective lists therapists at $40–70/session. — [Open Path](https://openpathcollective.org/clientvideo1/)
  - Typical out-of-pocket rates are about $100–200/session. — [Calmerry](https://calmerry.com/blog/therapy/out-of-pocket-therapy)
- **Single-session evidence:**
  - A Northwestern umbrella review (24 systematic reviews, 415 trials) found single-session interventions significantly reduce mental health problems for youth and adults. Jessica Schleider: "The most common number of sessions people access is one." — [Northwestern (Feb 2025)](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2025/02/single-session-interventions-significantly-reduce-mental-health-issues-for-youth-and-adults)
  - April 2026 AP coverage: many people "start and don't come back." — [The Star / AP (Apr 12, 2026)](https://www.thestar.com.my/lifestyle/health/2026/04/12/could-just-one-therapy-session-be-enough-to-help); [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/lifestyle/article/can-a-single-therapy-session-make-a-difference-experts-say-yes-with-the-right-mindset/)
- **Walk-in counselling (Canadian, non-US):** Foundry BC runs walk-in single-session counselling and reports one session as the most frequent course length. It is publicly funded. — [Foundry BC](https://foundrybc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/SERV_WalkInCounsellingOverviewV1.2_20211223.docx)
- **On-demand telehealth economics (Teladoc):**
  - In 2020, 79% of revenue came from subscription access fees and only 19% from visit fees. — [Teladoc 10-K FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477449/000155837021002118/tdoc-20201231x10k.htm)
  - Early utilization was 2.1% (2013) and 3.7% (2014). — [Rock Health S-1 analysis](https://rockhealth.com/?p=26433)

### Inferences
- Single-session therapy is clinically defensible, which supports the concept. But the businesses that deliver it at scale are institution-funded (employer coaching, public walk-in clinics), not consumer-paid.
- The successful on-demand layer used coaches rather than licensed therapists. Coaches can be pooled nationally and aren't bound by state licensure, which keeps on-call idle cost manageable. A licensed-therapist on-demand pool must be staffed state by state, which multiplies idle capacity.
- Teladoc's history shows that on-demand telehealth economics rest on subscription or access fees paid by institutions, not on per-visit revenue. Utilization of on-demand services is low, so per-visit-only revenue is thin.

### Gaps
- I couldn't verify any US startup built around one-off or drop-in licensed therapy (for travelers, or when a regular therapist is unavailable), nor its outcome. This is either a genuinely untried niche or one that failed quietly.
- I couldn't verify Sesame's same-day therapy pricing (one review site cites about $79) or Talkspace's or Amwell's current same-day therapy offerings.
- I found no data on what share of on-demand mental health requests are crisis-level, which matters for liability and triage design.

## 4. Marketplace economics, why insurance-enablement platforms were funded, and funding trends 2024–2026

### Takeaway
Cash-pay DTC therapy carries heavy advertising-driven acquisition costs and churn, and it is shrinking. Insurance-enablement platforms raised the big rounds because the payer is the customer: recurring, high-volume sessions, organic client demand from in-network search, and a supply-side moat in credentialing and billing. Their therapist-side margin is exposed to payer rate cuts. Digital health funding rebounded in 2025–2026, with mental health still the #1 funded clinical area, but capital is concentrated in a few $100M+ rounds and in AI.

### Cited Findings
**DTC acquisition costs and churn**
- BetterHelp's losses were attributed by Teladoc's CEO to customer acquisition and advertising costs (Q2 2024). — [Becker's Behavioral Health](https://www.beckersbehavioralhealth.com/behavioral-health-technology/losing-millions-teladoc-plans-betterhelp-pivot/)
- Teladoc's consolidated advertising and marketing spend was $124.0M in Q3 2026 (against $168.0M in Q3 2025). BetterHelp-specific ad spend is not broken out. — [Teladoc 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001477449/000147744926000050/tdoc-20260930xexx991.htm)
- BetterHelp spent an estimated $7.9M on podcast ads in February 2022 alone (Magellan AI estimate) and topped podcast advertiser rankings. — [Inside Radio](https://www.insideradio.com/free/betterhelp-again-outspends-all-others-to-top-february-s-list-of-top-podcast-advertisers/article_fff1d3b2-a684-11ec-9f7c-3f634af9ac2d.html)
- Self-pay user churn rose with consumer sentiment (2025). — [Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2025/10/30/teladoc-sees-in-network-coverage-as-lifeline-for-ongoing-betterhelp-weakness/)

**Payment model and survival**
- The Mentalium dataset (542 digital mental health organizations, 2000–2026) found:
  - Consumer-paid companies had a 53% shutdown or bankruptcy rate, against 21% where an institution paid.
  - B2C mortality was 53% against 24% for B2B.
  - Freemium models had 62% mortality, and one-time-purchase businesses 85%.
  - Methodology is not described, so treat these as directional. — [Behavioral Healthcare Network (Aug 31, 2026)](https://www.bhnet.org/78919/10-mental-health-startups-that-died-from-poor-financial-health/)

**Insurance-enablement funding**
- **Headway:** Series C $125M (October 2023) and Series D $100M (July 2024) at about a $2.3B valuation. — [NewMarketPitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/mental-health-top-startups-valuation)
- **Grow Therapy:** $150M Series D (March 2026) at about a $3B valuation, led by TCV. Rock Health cited it as an H1 2026 mental-health headline round. — [NewMarketPitch](https://newmarketpitch.com/blogs/news/mental-health-top-startups-valuation); [Rock Health H1 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/h1-2026-funding-and-market-overview-durable-roots-shifting-routes/)
- **Rula:** $143M Series C (July 2024, led by Hedosophia), about $263M raised in total. — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/rula-health/)
- **Alma:** about $222M raised over 8 rounds and valued at about $670–800M (2022 estimate). It was acquired by Spring Health (closed May 1, 2026). — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/alma-2/financials); [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2026/05/01/spring-health-alma-acquisition-lifelong-mental-health/)

**Therapist-side economics on these platforms**
- Headway pays about $107 on average for a 1-hour session (Single Aim's scraped data). — [Single Aim Health](https://www.singleaimhealth.com/news/how-much-does-headway-pay-therapists)
- Late 2024: Alma and Headway cut therapist pay in several states after Optum contract renewals. Therapists estimated annual losses of $6K–28K, and some stopped taking platform clients. — [ClearHealthCosts (Nov 2024)](https://clearhealthcosts.com/blog/2024/11/2-digital-mental-health-platforms-cut-pay-rates-for-therapists-with-unitedhealths-optum-stirring-anger/)
- 2026: Aetna reportedly cut rates for Alma clinicians. Small therapists report payer pressure to give up their own contracts and join enablement platforms "at the cost of autonomy." — [Behavioral Health Business (May 27, 2026)](https://bhbusiness.com/2026/05/27/small-providers-flock-to-therapist-enablement-platforms-at-the-cost-of-autonomy/); [ClearHealthCosts (Nov 2025)](https://clearhealthcosts.com/blog/2025/11/therapists-have-misgivings-on-the-platforms-alma-headway-etc-and-the-business-of-therapy/)

**Payer-model profitability**
- Talkspace became profitable (2025 net income $7.8M) as payor revenue grew to about 75% of revenue. — [Talkspace 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1803901/000119312526058146/talk-ex99_1.htm)

**Funding trends (Rock Health)**
- **2025:** $14.2B across 482 deals (+35% year over year, the highest since 2022).
  - $100M+ rounds were 42% of funding, and AI-enabled companies took 54% of funding.
  - There were 195 M&A deals (+61%) and five IPOs, including Hinge Health and Omada. — [Rock Health 2025 year-end](https://rockhealth.com/insights/2025-year-end-digital-health-funding-overview-a-tale-of-two-markets/); [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/digital-health-funding-2025-boosted-ai-rock-health/809449/)
- **2025 mental-health AI:** Rock Health flagged the rapid rise of mental-health AI chatbots, for example Slingshot AI's $93M debut. — [HIT Consultant (Dec 8, 2025)](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/12/08/rock-health-2025-report-mental-health-ai-and-longevity-tech-lead-innovation-curve/)
- **H1 2026:** $7.4B across 244 deals.
  - Mental health was the top-funded clinical indication for the seventh consecutive year (Talkiatry $210M, Grow $150M).
  - 20 rounds of $100M+ took 45% of capital. — [Rock Health H1 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/h1-2026-funding-and-market-overview-durable-roots-shifting-routes/)
- **Q3 2026:** $3.7B across 114 deals, bringing year-to-date to $11.1B across 358 deals, with 164 M&A deals year-to-date. — [Rock Health Q3 2026](https://rockhealth.com/insights/q3-2026-market-update-crystallizing-fifteen-years-of-digital-health/)

### Inferences
- Funded mental health companies have converged on payer-funded, recurring-care models (Grow, Headway, Rula, Talkiatry), and consolidation is under way (Spring–Alma, UHS–Talkspace). A one-off, cash-pay session marketplace runs against both the capital trend and the survival data. Consumer-paid mortality was 53%, and one-time-purchase mortality 85%, in the Mentalium set (directional).
- The enablement platforms' real product is a supply-side service: credentialing, billing and negotiated rates. A new entrant courting solo therapists would compete with free-to-therapist Headway, Grow and Alma, which already fill caseloads.
- Payer rate cuts flow straight through to therapists on platforms. That creates resentment a new entrant can exploit by being therapist-owned or therapist-aligned, but it also shows the platforms' margin depends on payer contracts the entrant won't have.
- **Rough DTC unit-economics check (my framing, not sourced data):** BetterHelp collects about $205/month per paying user and is barely profitable after advertising. A one-off session at about $100–200 cash yields roughly one month's revenue for one acquisition. Unless acquisition cost is near zero (for example, referral by the client's own therapist), paid acquisition for one-off clients is very likely uneconomic.

### Gaps
- I found no credible published CAC, LTV or average-tenure figures for BetterHelp, Talkspace or any therapy marketplace. One aggregator's "$200–300 CAC" claim was unsourced and is excluded.
- I didn't get primary-source take rates for Headway, Alma, Grow or Rula. Their revenue comes from the spread between payer rates and therapist payouts, and the exact spread is undisclosed.
- I found no Rock Health dollar total for mental health specifically in 2025 or Q3 2026.

## 5. Episodic and one-off business challenges, and comparable on-demand healthcare models

### Takeaway
On-demand healthcare models consistently fail when they rely on per-visit consumer demand and pay for idle capacity: Amazon Care (2022), Walmart Health (2024) and Forward (2024). They work when an institution with chronic, predictable need is the buyer: gig nursing marketplaces sell to facilities, and Teladoc relies on subscription access fees. Gig nursing also shows the regulatory cost: misclassification suits, DOL investigations, and reputational damage over pay opacity and deactivations.

### Cited Findings
**On-demand and retail care failures**
- **Amazon Care** shut down after December 31, 2022. Amazon said it was "not a complete enough offering for the large enterprise customers" it targeted. This came months after Amazon announced expansion to 20 more cities. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/amazon-care-shutting-down-end-2022-tech-giant-said-virtual-primary-care-business-wasnt); [CNBC (Aug 24, 2022)](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2022/08/24/amazon-is-shutting-down-amazon-care-telehealth-service.html)
- **Walmart Health** announced on April 30, 2024 that it would close all 51 health centers and its virtual care service. It cited a "challenging reimbursement environment" and rising operating costs that left the business unprofitable, a month after touting expansion. All centers had closed by June 28, 2024. — [Healthcare Brew](https://www.healthcare-brew.com/stories/2024/04/30/walmart-to-close-its-healthcare-clinics); [WUSF](https://wusf.org/economy-business/2024-06-27/walmart-says-its-health-clinics-and-telehealth-will-close-friday); [Fox 7](https://www.fox7austin.com/news/walmart-closing-51-health-centers-over-lack-of-profitability)
- **Forward** (membership primary care with AI "CarePods") shut down abruptly in November 2024 after raising roughly $400M (some reports say $650M+).
  - CarePods were expensive to build and patients did not adopt them as hoped.
  - Patients scrambled to retrieve records. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/primary-care-player-forward-shutters-after-raising-400m-rolling-out-carepods); [Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-news/patients-scramble-to-retrieve-records-after-startup-abruptly-closes/); [Business Insider](https://businessinsider.in/tech/news/inside-forwards-failed-attempt-to-revolutionize-the-doctors-office-with-ai/amp_articleshow/115266195.cms)

**Telehealth utilization**
- Teladoc's on-demand utilization was 2.1% in 2013 and 3.7% in 2014. Low utilization inflated the effective cost per visit to $145.66 in 2014. — [Rock Health S-1 analysis](https://rockhealth.com/?p=26433)
- By 2018, utilization was reported at about 10.8% (272 basis points above 8.1%). — [Motley Fool (Feb 2019)](https://www.fool.com/investing/2019/02/28/teladocs-rising-fees-and-visits-are-driving-profit.aspx)
- 79% of Teladoc's 2020 revenue was access fees. — [Teladoc 10-K FY2020](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1477449/000155837021002118/tdoc-20201231x10k.htm)

**Gig nursing marketplaces**
- Clipboard Health was valued at more than $1.3B and ShiftKey at more than $2B. Nurses on both are paid as contractors. — [The Markup via Truthout](https://truthout.org/articles/how-does-gig-nursing-work-affect-patients-nurses-and-health-care-facilities)
- Roosevelt Institute, "Uber for Nursing" (December 2024, 29 worker interviews):
  - Apps have workers bid for shifts by offering to work for the lowest wage.
  - Gig nurses averaged $59/hr against $45.42 for employed nurses, and gig nursing assistants $22 against $18.33, but before expenses.
  - Workers saw no transparency in algorithmic allocation, no orientation and no supervisor, plus deactivations and unpaid canceled shifts.
  - 14 of 29 said they couldn't live on app work alone. — [Roosevelt Institute](https://rooseveltinstitute.org/publications/uber-for-nursing/); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/nursing-has-become-a-new-frontier-for-gig-work-and-its-creating-the-same-issues-that-uber-drivers-face/)
- US DOL investigators announced more than a dozen misclassification and overtime cases involving gig nursing (around 2023). — [California Globe](https://californiaglobe.com/?p=52248); [Thomson Reuters Foundation via PressReader (Dec 14, 2023)](https://www.pressreader.com/south-africa/business-day/20231214/281672554731525)
- 2026: a staffing agency (NPH Medical Services) is suing Clipboard Health and a co-defendant over the contractor model. A July 24, 2026 discovery order compels disclosure of nurse wages, client fees and contractor-cost math.
  - Clipboard also has a prior California wage-and-hour settlement (Williams and Minor v. TwoMagnets Inc. dba Clipboard Health).
  - Single outlet; not independently verified. — [RuntimeWire (Jul 24, 2026)](https://runtimewire.com/article/clipboard-health-lawsuit-forces-open-the-economics-of-gig-nursing)
- Separately, CNAs sued a facility (Medicalodges) that staffs largely through ShiftKey. — [Staffing Industry Analysts](https://cwstrategies.staffingindustry.com/?p=18459)

### Inferences
**Transferable lessons**
1. **The buyer must have recurring, predictable need.** Facilities need shifts filled every week, which makes nurse marketplaces viable. A traveler needing therapy is rare and unpredictable, so per-event consumer demand is the weak side. A coverage network for solo therapists has a better buyer: each therapist needs coverage several times a year (vacations, illness) and has a standing reason to belong. But volume per member is still low, so it likely needs a membership or subscription fee rather than per-event revenue (the Teladoc lesson).
2. **Don't pay for idle on-call capacity.** Gig-shift models push idle cost onto workers, who are paid only when booked, and that is exactly what triggered pay-opacity and deactivation backlash. A therapy equivalent should let therapists post the slots they already have (late cancellations), not ask them to sit on call unpaid.
3. **Worker classification is a live legal risk.** Shift-bidding, algorithmic allocation and platform control over clinicians drew DOL and private suits in nursing. A therapy platform that sets prices, assigns clients and controls scheduling invites the same scrutiny.
4. **Big balance sheets weren't enough.** Amazon, Walmart and Forward, with roughly $400M+, all failed at on-demand or retail care. Distribution and capital didn't fix reimbursement and utilization economics.
5. **Abrupt closure damages patients.** Forward patients scrambled for records, and Mindstrong ended care on about two months' notice. A one-off care network needs a records and continuity plan from day one, especially since the client's regular therapist must get the session note.

### Gaps
- Search budget ran out before I could source locum tenens economics (agency markups, fill rates, physician on-call pay) or urgent care and retail clinic utilization data. These need follow-up.
- I didn't verify take rates or markups for Clipboard, ShiftKey, Nomad Health or CareRev. The RuntimeWire article references but does not report them.
- I found no data on late-cancellation rates in outpatient therapy or how often cancelled slots could be refilled. That is the core assumption for the slot-filling idea.

## 6. Recurring controversy themes a new entrant should avoid by design

### Takeaway
Six controversy patterns recur:
1. Ad-tracker data sharing (BetterHelp, Cerebral, Crisis Text Line)
2. Volume-driven clinician incentives that compromise care (Done, Cerebral)
3. Subscription and cancellation dark patterns (Cerebral)
4. Platform squeezing of clinician pay (Optum and Aetna cuts flowing through Alma and Headway; gig nursing)
5. Unregulated AI "therapy" (state bans since 2025; Character.AI lawsuits; Woebot's regulatory exit)
6. Abrupt shutdowns that strand patients

Each one can be designed out up front.

### Cited Findings
- **Data sharing with advertisers:**
  - BetterHelp paid $7.8M (FTC, March 2023) for sharing emails, IPs and intake answers with Facebook, Snapchat, Criteo and Pinterest. — [HealthITSecurity](https://healthitsecurity.com/news/ftc-proposes-7.8m-settlement-with-betterhelp-over-improper-health-data-sharing)
  - Cerebral (2024) shared data on about 3.2M users with ad platforms through trackers and is permanently banned from using health data for ads. — [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2024/06/11/cerebral-settles-ftc-charges-over-data-privacy-and-deceptive-practices)
  - Crisis Text Line ended data sharing with its for-profit spinoff Loris.ai in January 2022, three days after POLITICO reported it. An FCC commissioner urged an FTC probe. — [Popular Science](https://www.popsci.com/technology/crisis-text-line-stops-sharing-data-loris-ai/); [Becker's](https://beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/suicide-hotline-shares-data-with-own-for-profit-raising-ethics-concerns.html); [Benton Institute](https://www.benton.org/headlines/fcc-commissioner-carr-calls-ftc-probe-crisis-text-line)
- **Volume-driven clinical incentives:**
  - Done paid clinicians per refill processed, and some renewals took under a minute. — [Wikipedia (citing DOJ)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Done_Global)
  - Cerebral tracked the share of controlled-substance prescriptions going to ADHD patients without comorbidities as a business metric. — [DOJ EDNY](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/telehealth-company-cerebral-agrees-pay-over-36-million-connection-business-practices)
- **Cancellation practices:** the Cerebral order requires a simple cancellation method. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/regulatory/cerebral-agrees-7m-settlement-ftc-doj-over-user-data-sharing-cancellation-practices)
- **Clinician pay and gig-ification:**
  - Optum-driven pay cuts on Alma and Headway (late 2024). — [ClearHealthCosts](https://clearhealthcosts.com/blog/2024/11/unitedhealth-optum-pay-cut-makes-clinicians-reassess-value-of-tech-mental-health-platforms/)
  - Aetna cuts and loss of autonomy for small practices (2026). — [Behavioral Health Business](https://bhbusiness.com/2026/05/27/small-providers-flock-to-therapist-enablement-platforms-at-the-cost-of-autonomy/)
  - Gig nursing: lowest-bid shift pricing, deactivations and misclassification probes. — [Roosevelt Institute](https://rooseveltinstitute.org/publications/uber-for-nursing/)
- **AI therapy regulation:**
  - Illinois' Wellness and Oversight for Psychological Resources Act (signed August 2025) bans AI from providing therapy or making therapeutic decisions, with fines up to $10,000. Administrative AI use is allowed. — [Marketplace (Sep 1, 2025)](https://www.marketplace.org/story/2025/09/01/new-illinois-law-restricts-use-of-ai-in-psychotherapy); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/healthcare/1671306/new-illinois-law-restricts-use-of-ai-in-mental-health-therapy)
  - Utah and Nevada passed earlier 2025 restrictions. New York requires AI companions to refer suicidal users to hotlines (effective November 5, 2025). — [AOL/news syndication](https://www.aol.com/illinois-becomes-third-state-restrict-035404378.html)
  - Character.AI faces multiple lawsuits over harmful chatbot interactions with minors, including a 14-year-old who expressed suicidal thoughts. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ai-therapists-banned-illinois)
- **Leadership and governance:** Talkspace's 2021 leadership exodus included a COO resignation after a conduct review at a company offsite, plus securities-law investigations. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/tech/amid-talkspace-leadership-exodus-and-drop-stock-law-firms-investigating-potential-securities)
- **Abrupt shutdowns:**
  - Forward patients scrambled for records (November 2024). — [Becker's ASC](https://www.beckersasc.com/asc-news/patients-scramble-to-retrieve-records-after-startup-abruptly-closes/)
  - Mindstrong ended patient services on March 10, 2023 after a January announcement. — [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/mental-health-platform-mindstrong-close-hq-lay-128-employees)
  - Woebot gave users until June 30, 2025 to download data. — [Telehealth.org](https://telehealth.org/news/ai-psychotherapy-shutdown-what-woebots-exit-signals-for-clinicians/)

### Inferences
**Design rules for a one-off or coverage-network therapy app**
- **No third-party ad pixels or SDKs on any page or screen that touches intake, matching or booking.** Treat even "which therapist did you view" as health data. Marketing can't use retargeting on health signals. This is the single most-penalized pattern.
- **Don't pay therapists or rank them by volume of one-off sessions in a way that rewards speed over clinical judgment.** Done's per-refill pay is the cautionary tale. Include structured risk screening before booking a one-off session, and an escalation path (988 and crisis resources) when a one-off client presents at high risk.
- **Pricing and cancellation:** per-session, transparent pricing with no auto-renewing subscriptions for clients. If therapists pay a coverage-network membership, offer one-click cancellation.
- **Therapist economics:**
  - Let therapists set their own rates and keep control of scheduling.
  - Avoid lowest-bid auctions and opaque algorithmic allocation.
  - Pay for completed sessions promptly, and never deactivate without due process.
  - Posting existing late-cancel slots avoids the unpaid on-call problem that triggered gig-nursing backlash.
  - Classification counsel is needed if the platform sets prices or assigns clients.
- **Continuity by design:** a coverage session should produce a note that goes back to the client's regular therapist with consent. Keep a records-export and wind-down plan so that even if the company fails, no client is stranded like Forward's patients.
- **AI positioning:** keep AI to admin and matching and out of therapeutic decisions. Illinois-style laws make "AI therapist" features a liability in several states.
- **Branding:** drop "Tinder for therapy" externally. It signals casual selection and invites the trivialization and boundary criticism these controversies amplify.

### Gaps
- I didn't research HIPAA or FTC Health Breach Notification Rule specifics for a marketplace that may not be a covered entity. Legal review is needed on whether the app is a HIPAA business associate, a covered entity, or subject to the FTC Health Breach Notification Rule.
- State licensure limits on treating traveling clients across state lines (PSYPACT, Counseling Compact status) are central to the "traveler" use case. They are outside this note's scope and were not researched here.
- I found no reporting on malpractice or liability claims specific to single-session or coverage therapy, or on insurance coverage of one-off out-of-network sessions.
