# Client-Side Access Gaps in US Mental-Health Care: Unmet Need, Barriers, Matching Pain and Gap Sessions (as of Nov 2026)

Notes on evidence quality: the **2024 NSDUH** (SAMHSA, released July 2025) is the latest federal treatment and unmet-need data I found. I read its detailed tables directly, so those figures are primary. I could not open the Senate Finance PDF or the NAMI PDF (HTTP 403), so the figures from those documents come from press releases and trade coverage. Reddit pages could not be fetched directly. Lived-experience themes come from forum/review pages and aggregator analyses that surfaced in search, and they are labeled **[Anecdotal]**. Numbers from peer-reviewed, government or polling sources are labeled **[Stat]**.

## 1. Unmet need: how many people with mental illness get no treatment, and why?

### Takeaway
About half of US adults with any mental illness (AMI) got no treatment in 2024: 29.5M of 61.5M. Most of those untreated adults (79%) did not perceive a need for care. Among the roughly 6M who did perceive a need, the top reasons were self-reliance ("should handle it on my own," 71%), cost (65%), not knowing how or where to get care (49%), not being ready (48%), lack of time (48%) and not finding the right professional (45%). A lack of openings was cited much less often (16.5%). Among adolescents, privacy and stigma lead, ahead of cost.

### Cited Findings
**Adults: [Stat]**
- 2024: 61.5M adults (about 23%) had AMI. 52.1% (32.0M) received any mental health treatment in the past year. Among adults with serious mental illness (SMI), 70.8% (10.3M of 14.6M) received treatment. Because the 2024 questionnaire changed, these treatment estimates are **not comparable** with 2022 or 2023. — [SAMHSA 2024 NSDUH press release](https://www.samhsa.gov/newsroom/press-announcements/20250728/samhsa-releases-annual-national-survey-on-drug-use-and-health); [2024 NSDUH Annual National Report (detailed tables)](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm)
- How adults with AMI got treated in 2024: 34.7% any outpatient care, 27.0% in a therapist/psychologist/psychiatrist office, **32.6% via telehealth**, 39.9% prescription medication, 16.3% in a general medical clinic, 3.7% in an ER and 3.6% inpatient. For SMI the figures were 53.3% outpatient, 48.3% telehealth and 7.6% ER. — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm)
- Perceived unmet need among adults with AMI who got **no** treatment (about 29.5M): **21.0% (about 6.06M)** perceived an unmet need. 2.8% sought treatment and did not get it, 18.3% thought they should get treatment but did not seek it, and **79.0% did not perceive a need**. — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm); consistent with [National Council summary](https://www.thenationalcouncil.org/numbers-dont-lie-2024-data-shows-our-work-isnt-done/)
- Reasons for not getting treatment among adults with AMI and a perceived unmet need, 2024 (multiple answers allowed): thought they should handle it on their own **71.0%**; thought it would cost too much **65.2%**; did not know how or where to get treatment **49.2%**; not ready to start **48.1%**; did not have enough time **47.9%**; could not find the program or professional they wanted **45.0%**; insurance would not pay enough **39.9%**; no insurance coverage for mental health **39.1%**; did not think treatment would help 36.9%; thought they would be told to take medication 34.4%; transportation, childcare or appointment-time problems **26.4%**; worried what people would think 26.4%; privacy worries 23.4%; afraid of being committed 19.6%; thought no one would care 17.5%; **no openings 16.5%**; family or religious disapproval 13.4%; feared losing job, home or children 12.5%. — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm)
- A different survey with a different measure, CDC's NHIS (2024), found that **14.0% of adults received counseling or therapy from a mental health professional** in the past 12 months and 19.3% took medication for their mental health. This should not be compared directly with NSDUH. — [CDC NCHS Data Brief 564](https://cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db564.htm)
- Mental Health America's 2025 report (the latest MHA edition I found): about 1 in 4 adults with AMI reported an unmet need for care in 2022–2023; there is about **1 provider per 320 people** (MHA's own ratio); and the South and more rural states have the largest access gaps. — [Healthline on MHA 2025](https://www.healthline.com/health-news/best-worst-states-mental-health-america)

**Youth (ages 12–17): [Stat]**
- 2024: **60.6%** of adolescents with a past-year major depressive episode (MDE) received treatment. By setting: 37.0% in a therapist's office, **29.8% at a school counseling center**, **33.2% via telehealth**, 31.2% prescription medication and 7.3% in an ER. Adolescent MDE prevalence fell from 20.8% (2021) to 15.4% (2024). — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm); [APR coverage](https://www.apr.org/science-health/2025-08-04/teen-suicide-is-on-the-decline-new-federal-data-shows)
- Among adolescents with MDE who were **not** treated, 42.4% perceived an unmet need. Their reasons: worried what people would think **70.5%**; worried about privacy **64.9%**; didn't know how or where to get help **51.2%**; not ready 45.7%; cost 39.1%; not enough time 31.5%; couldn't find the professional they wanted 28.6%; transport or appointment times 22.6%; no openings 8.3%. — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm)

### Inferences
- The largest group of untreated people (about 23M adults with AMI and no perceived need) are not shopping for therapy, so a matching app will not reach them. The addressable "actively frustrated" pool is the roughly 6M untreated adults who perceive a need, plus people already in care who have access problems.
- In that pool, **navigation failures** are very common: 49% didn't know how or where to get care and 45% couldn't find the professional they wanted. **Cost** is cited even more often (65%). Both are core matching and pricing problems. **Capacity** ("no openings," 16.5%) is cited far less, which suggests the problem is less a lack of supply than a failure to find and afford the supply that exists.
- "Not enough time" (48%) and "appointment times/transport/childcare" (26%) point to scheduling flexibility, including evenings, short-notice slots and virtual sessions, as a real lever. This supports on-demand availability more than the travel story does.
- Telehealth is already mainstream (about a third of treated adults and teens use it). Virtual one-off sessions are therefore not a behavioral leap for clients.

### Gaps
- The 2024 NSDUH reasons table does not report perceived unmet need among adults who **did** receive treatment (for example, people whose care was inadequate or interrupted). I could not find that figure for 2024.
- I could not find an MHA 2026 "State of Mental Health in America" edition. Coverage of a May 2026 report saying every state lacks enough providers did not name the source.

## 2. Wait times and directories: how long is the wait, and what do secret-shopper and ghost-network studies show?

### Takeaway
Secret-shopper studies consistently find that only about 1 in 5 directory listings leads to an appointment. That held for the 2023 Senate Finance study of Medicare Advantage (18%) and a 2024 JAMA study of Medicaid psychiatric prescribers (17.8%). Waits run about 1–2 months when an appointment is offered. Capacity is improving somewhat: 46% of psychologists had no openings in 2025, down from 56% in 2023. Federal wait-time standards (10 business days for behavioral health on the ACA exchanges) now exist but are only beginning to be enforced.

### Cited Findings
**Statistics [Stat]**
- **Senate Finance Committee secret-shopper report (May 2023):** staff called listed mental health providers in 12 Medicare Advantage plans across 6 states, trying to book for a hypothetical relative with depression. They got an appointment only **18%** of the time, and in Oregon they got **zero** appointments. Summaries report that about 33% of the 120 providers contacted had inaccurate numbers or did not call back, and that more than 80% of listings were inaccurate or unavailable. These tallies measure different things. The committee also noted that CMS does not routinely audit MA directories. — [Senate Finance press release](https://www.finance.senate.gov/chairmans-news/wyden-calls-for-action-to-get-rid-of-ghost-networks-releases-secret-shopper-study); [Report PDF](https://www.finance.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/050323%20Ghost%20Network%20Hearing%20-%20Secret%20Shopper%20Study%20Report.pdf) (not directly retrievable; figures via [Becker's Payer](https://www.beckerspayer.com/?p=10082) and [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/healthcarepayers/news/366603542/Medicare-Advantage-Provider-Directories-Are-Inaccurate-Study-Finds))
- **JAMA (July 2024), Medicaid managed-care psychiatric prescribers in NYC, LA, Chicago and Phoenix:** only **17.8%** of listed in-network clinicians were reachable, accepted Medicaid and could see a new patient. NYC had the best availability (36% of calls led to an appointment) with a median wait of **28 days**. In LA only 15% of calls succeeded, with a median wait of **64 days**. — [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2821639); [News-Medical summary](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240731/Study-reveals-challenges-in-accessing-psychiatric-care-for-Medicaid-enrollees.aspx)
- **Oregon Medicaid, Health Affairs (2022), using 2018 claims:** **67% of listed mental health prescribers and 59% of non-prescribers were "phantom" listings** (they saw no Medicaid patients), compared with 54% for primary care. Phantom networks were larger in mental health than in primary care. — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/study-finds-rampant-phantom-docs-medicaid-network-directories-oregon); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20220705/Phantom-networks-undermine-access-to-mental-health-care-for-patients-most-in-need-study-says.aspx)
- **Health Affairs (2020) survey of patients:** 44% had used a mental health directory. **53% of those hit inaccuracies**, and people who hit an inaccuracy were **4x more likely to receive an out-of-network bill**. — [APA testimony to Senate Finance, May 2023](https://www.finance.senate.gov/download/05032023-resnick-testimony&download=1)
- **APA Practitioner Pulse surveys (psychologists):** in 2025, **46% had no openings for new patients**, down from 53% in 2024 and 56% in 2023. **87% said they could see new patients within 3 months**, and 45% reported that patients' symptoms were getting more severe. — [EurekAlert (APA 2025 release)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1109019); [APA 2024 report](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/practitioner/2024); [APA 2023 report](https://apa.org/pubs/reports/practitioner/2023-psychologist-reach-limits)
- **Regulatory standards:** starting January 1, 2025, CMS requires ACA federally facilitated exchange plans to meet appointment wait-time standards of **10 business days for behavioral health** and 15 business days for routine primary care, verified by third-party surveys. A May 2024 Medicaid managed-care rule requires annual, publicly reported independent secret-shopper surveys, enforced from July 2027 onward. — [Forvis Mazars (Aug 2026)](https://www.forvismazars.us/forsights/2026/08/ffe-appointment-wait-time-standards-how-qhps-can-succeed); [Atlas Systems](https://www.atlassystems.com/blog/cms-appointment-wait-time) (vendor and compliance sources)
- Industry, not mental-health-specific: ECG's secret shopper of about 4,000 practices in 23 cities (2023 data, published 2024) found an average wait of 38 days to the third next available new-patient appointment across specialties. — [Becker's](https://beckershospitalreview.com/strategy/the-waiting-game-new-patient-appointment-access-for-us-physicians.html)

**Lived experience [Anecdotal]**
- Forum posts describe insurer portals with outdated contact details, therapists who never return calls, full voicemail boxes and full caseloads. People call the search "exhausting," especially "when you're at a breaking point." — [Team Blind thread](https://www.teamblind.com/post/getting-mental-healththerapists-is-so-freaking-difficult-fttsitjv); [Lemmy thread on practitioners not calling back](https://next.lemm.ee/comment/7320215); [MyPTSD thread](https://www.myptsd.com/threads/therapist-hasnt-responded-in-almost-two-weeks.100846/); [HealthUnlocked post](https://healthunlocked.com/anxiety-depression-support/posts/147271603/difficulty-finding-a-therapist)

### Inferences
- The binding problem is **verified, bookable availability**, not the number of listings. A product that shows only providers with confirmed open slots, accepted insurance and real response SLAs attacks the exact failure that secret shoppers measure. That failure is about 80% of listings in public-payer networks.
- Capacity is loosening at the margin: fewer psychologists report no openings each year, and 87% can see a new patient within 3 months. That favors a matching and booking layer over a pure supply play. A 2–3 month wait is still a long time to bridge, though, which is a real "gap session" opportunity.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 national secret-shopper study of commercial-plan **therapists** (as opposed to psychiatrists or Medicaid prescribers). Most rigorous wait-time data covers psychiatric prescribers.
- I did not retrieve the full Senate Finance report, so the 33% and 80% tallies rest on secondary summaries.
- I found no 2026 results from the new CMS exchange wait-time surveys.

## 3. Cost: what people pay out of pocket, what goes wrong with insurance, and where parity enforcement stands (2024 rule to 2026)

### Takeaway
Cost is the most commonly cited structural barrier (65% of adults with unmet need). A self-pay session averages about $140–$180, and behavioral health visits are several times more likely than medical visits to be out-of-network because many therapists don't take insurance. The 2024 MHPAEA final rule's new protections were never enforced. The Trump administration paused enforcement in May 2025. In 2026 it moved to replace the rule and narrowed DOL enforcement to three areas (exclusions, medical-necessity/utilization management and network adequacy). The federal parity lever is therefore weaker in 2026 than planned.

### Cited Findings
**Out-of-pocket and coverage [Stat]**
- Average private-practice session price without insurance was **$143.26** (2024 analysis of more than 175,000 therapists). — [Thriveworks](https://thriveworks.com/?p=314738). A 2022 Zencare study found **$182**. — [Business Wire / Zencare](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220419005728/en). The highest state averages in 2025 were North Dakota at $227 and Alaska at $212. — [SimplePractice](https://www.simplepractice.com/blog/average-therapy-session-fees-by-state/)
- Milliman claims analyses: in 2013–2015, **18.7% of behavioral health office visits were out-of-network vs. 3.7%** of primary medical/surgical visits. By 2017, behavioral health office visits were about **5.4x** more likely to be out-of-network (Moneygeek cites 5.2x, so the ratio differs by source). In some states (for example New Jersey, about 41–45% depending on source) close to half of behavioral health office visits were out-of-network. Medical providers were paid about 20% more than behavioral providers for similar office-visit codes. — [KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/if-your-insurer-covers-few-therapists-is-that-really-mental-health-parity/); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/payer-issues/behavioral-care-more-likely-to-be-out-of-network-than-physical-care.html); [Moneygeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/health/why-in-network-mental-health-care-is-hard-to-find/)
- Why therapists stay out of network: in APA's 2025 survey, **three-quarters of psychologists** cited insufficient reimbursement as a primary reason for not accepting insurance, 57% cited payer administrative issues and 43% cited payment reliability. — [EurekAlert (APA 2025)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1109019). A 2025 APA figure that **38% of psychologists accept no insurance** comes secondhand. — [Moneygeek](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/health/why-in-network-mental-health-care-is-hard-to-find/)
- Grow Therapy/YouGov poll (weighted, n=2,424, Oct 24–28, 2025): **20% of US adults have delayed or avoided therapy because of insurance or open-enrollment difficulties**, 32% say copays, deductibles and out-of-pocket costs are the most confusing part of coverage, only 31% are "very confident" they can pick a plan that meets their mental health needs, and 26% of parents struggled to find affordable therapy for a child. — [Grow Therapy](https://growtherapy.com/blog/mental-health-coverage-survey) (vendor-commissioned)
- Directory errors push people out of network: patients who encountered directory inaccuracies were 4x more likely to receive an out-of-network bill (Health Affairs 2020). — [APA testimony](https://www.finance.senate.gov/download/05032023-resnick-testimony&download=1)

**Parity enforcement timeline [Stat/regulatory]**
- **Sept 2024:** the Biden administration finalized the 2024 MHPAEA rule (NQTL comparative analyses, network-adequacy outcome data). **January 2025:** the ERISA Industry Committee (ERIC) sued, and the case was stayed with status reports due every 90 days. **May 15, 2025:** DOL, HHS and Treasury announced **non-enforcement** of the 2024 rule's provisions that are new relative to the 2013 rule. The statute and the 2013 rule still apply. — [ABA Health Law eSource](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/health_law/resources/esource/2025/status-2024-final-mental-health-parity-rule/); [Husch Blackwell (July 2025)](https://www.huschblackwell.com/newsandinsights/mhpaea-july-2025-update-what-employers-and-plans-need-to-know-about-federal-non-enforcement)
- **March 30, 2026 (single secondary source, unverified):** in a joint status report, the Departments reportedly said they would **no longer defend the 2024 rule** and would issue a **new final rule by Dec 31, 2026**. — reported in law-firm commentary surfaced in search (see [Reed Smith, "MHPAEA final rule inches closer to chopping block"](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/mhpaea-final-rule-inches-closer-to-chopping-block/)); verify against the court docket.
- **Sept 8, 2026:** DOL issued **Field Assistance Bulletin 2026-03** and a new MHPAEA Self-Compliance Tool. These narrow NQTL comparative-analysis enforcement to three categories: (1) treatment exclusions, (2) medical-necessity and utilization management including prior authorization, and (3) **network adequacy**. Plans may keep using proprietary clinical guidelines if they apply them comparably. Commentators call this a "lighter touch … for now." Mercer frames it as a sharpened focus on behavioral health access. — [Mayer Brown (Sept 11, 2026)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/dol-announces-new-enforcement-policy-for-mental-health-parity--nonquantitative-treatment-limitation-requirements); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/dol-signals-a-lighter-touch-on-mental-health-parity-enforcement-for-now); [Mercer](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/us-health-news/employers-should-review-benefits-given-new-mental-health-parity-guidance/)

### Inferences
- Insurance-driven fixes (parity, network adequacy) are unlikely to close the in-network gap in 2026–2027. Cash-pay or out-of-network-reimbursed care will remain common. For a one-off-session product, price is the key variable, and a superbill/out-of-network reimbursement flow plus transparent per-session pricing addresses a widely felt confusion (Grow/YouGov).
- DOL's continued focus on **network adequacy** is a small tailwind for B2B sales to plans and employers that need to show real access, for example verified availability data.

### Gaps
- No primary confirmation of the March 30, 2026 "no longer defend" filing, and no confirmation of whether a replacement final rule had been published as of early November 2026.
- I found no 2025–2026 nationally representative data on actual out-of-pocket spend per therapy episode for insured patients, as opposed to list prices.

## 4. Matching experience: recurring frustrations, fit, identity matching and quitting or switching

### Takeaway
The quantitative signal is clear on three points. Therapists respond unevenly to inquiries, with documented race and class bias in callbacks. Many therapy episodes end early (about 30–50% dropout, concentrated in the first sessions). People often try several therapists before one fits (one poll put the average at about 3). Qualitative signals (forums, app reviews) add search exhaustion, unanswered inquiries, consult-call fatigue, billing friction on subscription apps and pain when switching after a bad fit.

### Cited Findings
**Statistics [Stat]**
- **Audit study (Kugelmass, Journal of Health and Social Behavior, 2016; 320 NYC therapists from an insurer directory, same insurance for all callers):** callback rates were about **51%** for middle-class white callers, 49% for middle-class Black callers, 45% for working-class white callers and **34%** for working-class Black callers. Appointment offers went to 28% of middle-class white callers vs. 17% of middle-class Black callers, and to about 8% of working-class callers of either race. The author attributes this to bias rather than finances. — [American Sociological Association](https://www.asanet.org/news_item/study-finds-evidence-racial-and-class-discrimination-among-psychotherapists/). A JBHE summary reports different subgroup numbers (Black men called back 13%), so check the paper. — [JBHE](https://jbhe.com/2016/06/princeton-university-study-suggests-racial-bias-in-access-to-mental-health-care)
- **Dropout:** the classic meta-analysis (Wierzbicki & Pekarik, 1993, 125 studies) found about **47%** premature dropout, and later reviews put typical rates at **30–50%**. Dropout clusters in the **first sessions**, and therapists differ significantly in their dropout rates even after adjusting for patient severity. — [Dropout meta-analysis (ISPA copy)](https://clinica.ispa.pt/sites/default/files/37._dropout_meta_analysis.pdf); [Swift & Greenberg treatment-by-disorder meta-analysis](https://clinica.ispa.pt/sites/default/files/38._a_treatment_by_disorder_meta-analysis_of_dropout_from_psychotherapy.pdf); [Therapist effects on dropout](https://metatoc.com/papers/52119-therapist-effects-on-and-predictors-of-non-consensual-dropout-in-psychotherapy)
- **Number of therapists tried:** a OnePoll/Mindstrong survey of 2,000 adults (circa 2022; methodology not verifiable) found the average American sees **3 therapists** before finding a match, and **72%** said searching for the right therapist overwhelms them. — [OnePoll/Mindstrong via Dailymotion](https://www.dailymotion.com/video/x8aht5m) (weak sourcing)
- **Platform switching:** a self-selected survey by a competitor directory found 48% of BetterHelp respondents described switching therapists as "painful." It is biased and non-representative. — [First Session](https://www.firstsession.com/resources/betterhelp-reviews-survey-results)

**Lived experience [Anecdotal]**
- **Unanswered outreach and exhaustion:** posters report contacting several therapists, with most not responding, outdated portal numbers and full voicemails. They describe repeating their story as "exhausting." Commenters recommend identity-focused directories (Inclusive Therapists, TherapyDen) and asking psychiatrists or past therapists for referrals. — [Team Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/getting-mental-healththerapists-is-so-freaking-difficult-fttsitjv); [Lemmy](https://next.lemm.ee/comment/7320215); [MySupportForums "why so hard"](https://mysupportforums.org/psychotherapy/552000-why-so-hard.html)
- **How many tries:** in an informal forum poll, 52% had seen 0–3 therapists before connecting with one, and about 13% had seen 8–10. One poster reported interviewing about 18–20 therapists. — [MySupportForums poll](https://mysupportforums.org/psychotherapy/497933-how-many.html) (not representative)
- **Subscription apps (BetterHelp) in 1–3★ reviews:** therapist churn, missed appointments and therapist no-shows, billing and subscription disputes, refund difficulty when a match fails, and matching delays (one complaint says matching took more than 2 weeks while billing continued). BetterHelp says switching is free and unlimited and rematching takes "a few hours to a few days." Its Trustpilot score is around 3.8–4.0 on more than 9K reviews. — [Kimola Trustpilot analysis](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-betterhelp-customer-feedback-analysis-report-trustpilot-en-us-148812); [Kimola App Store analysis](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-betterhelp-therapy-app-user-feedback-report-app-store-us-151342); [ComplaintsBoard](https://www.complaintsboard.com/betterhelp-b126477); [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.betterhelp.com?page=2)

### Inferences
- Matching fails at three points that a product could instrument: **(1) response** (does the therapist reply or book within X hours?), **(2) early fit** (dropout and switching cluster in sessions 1–3) and **(3) bias in who gets a callback**. Structured, bookable intake with no voicemail tag, plus a low-friction "try another therapist" path, maps onto the documented failure modes.
- Bias in therapist-controlled callbacks is a point in favor of instant booking against posted availability over inquiry-based directories (the Psychology Today-style model).
- A low-commitment single session as a **"fit test"** could plausibly cut the cost of the 3-therapist search. That is an inference; I found no trial of it.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/TalkTherapy, r/therapy, r/college) could not be fetched directly, so I have no systematic theme counts. The forum themes above come from other forums and threads that surfaced in search.
- I found no rigorous US data on the share of clients who switch therapists because of fit, on how often identity matching (race, LGBTQ+, faith, language) is requested or fulfilled, or on consult-call volume per successful match.

## 5. Gap scenarios: how common are they (travel, students moving between states, therapist away, between therapists, waitlist, one session for one issue)?

### Takeaway
I found **no quantitative data on how often clients are traveling or have a therapist who is unavailable**. The evidence that exists suggests those scenarios are real but small and often solved already: by the client's own therapist via telehealth (if licensure allows), by covering colleagues, or by employer platforms. By contrast, the waitlist gap (46% of psychologists have no openings, and waits run 1–2+ months) and the "one session for one issue" demand (the modal number of sessions attended is one, and single-session interventions have evidence) are larger and better supported. The hardest structural constraint on any cross-state one-off session is **licensure in the client's physical location**.

### Cited Findings
**One-off / single-session demand [Stat]**
- Internationally, the **modal (most common) number of therapy sessions attended is one** (Dryden). — [Taylor & Francis chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9780429489648-6/modal-number-therapy-sessions-internationally-one-majority-people-attend-one-session-satisfied-windy-dryden). In an Italian three-service record review, one session was the most frequent count (124 of 476 clients, 26%). In a related survey, 52% of 85 single-session clients said one session was enough. — [Università Cattolica](https://publires.unicatt.it/it/publications/examining-the-incidence-and-clients-experiences-of-single-session/) (non-US, small). Secondhand: 20% to nearly 60% of people who see a therapist go only once. — [Oprah.com](https://www.oprah.com/health_wellness/what-is-single-session-therapy) (unverified)
- **Efficacy:** Schleider & Weisz's 2017 meta-analysis of youth single-session interventions found an overall effect of g = 0.32, with no difference between self-administered and therapist-delivered sessions. The depression effect was smaller and nonsignificant (reported as 0.21 or 0.18 depending on summary), and effects fade over weeks to months. — [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/handle/1/41292903). A 2025 Northwestern umbrella review covered 24 systematic reviews and 415 trials across youth and adults, and **83% of the reviews reported positive effects** on at least one outcome, including depression and anxiety. — [Northwestern News (Feb 2025)](https://news.northwestern.edu/stories/2025/02/single-session-interventions-significantly-reduce-mental-health-issues-for-youth-and-adults)

**Waitlist gap [Stat]**
- In 2025, 46% of psychologists had no openings and 87% could see new patients within 3 months. — [EurekAlert (APA 2025)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1109019). Medicaid psychiatric waits had medians of 28–64 days by city. — [News-Medical on JAMA 2024](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240731/Study-reveals-challenges-in-accessing-psychiatric-care-for-Medicaid-enrollees.aspx)

**Therapist away (vacation, parental leave, sick) [practice guidance / Anecdotal]**
- Guidance for therapists on leave describes three options: pausing practice, referring clients to a covering colleague, or **discharging clients**. Rula's guidance says discharge "may be appropriate" during extended leave, such as maternity leave or extended vacations. APA advises introducing a covering therapist early and notes that some clients experience a leave as abandonment. Headway advises that a leave notice tell clients whom to contact for urgent needs. — [Rula therapist support](https://therapistsupport.rula.com/hc/en-us/articles/46641831224603-Taking-time-off-Ethical-and-clinical-best-practices-for-supporting-patients); [APA Monitor (2014)](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2014/03/baby); [Headway](https://headway.co/resources/therapist-maternity-leave)
- Short vacations are typically covered informally by a colleague. That claim rests on practitioner accounts, not data. — [Rula](https://therapistsupport.rula.com/hc/en-us/articles/46641831224603-Taking-time-off-Ethical-and-clinical-best-practices-for-supporting-patients)

**Traveling clients and licensure [Stat/regulatory]**
- Clinicians generally must be licensed where the **client is physically located** during the session. — [Spring Health help center](https://careteam.springhealth.com/hc/en-us/articles/24232935255444-Accessing-Care-While-Out-of-State-or-Traveling); [HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2023/02/28/consumers-prefer-telehealth-to-in-person-visits-while-traveling). Spring Health, an employer mental-health benefit, already publishes a help article on getting care while out of state or traveling, which suggests incumbents handle this case.
- **PSYPACT** (psychologists) covers 40+ jurisdictions and permits telepsychology and temporary in-person practice across member states. Vendor sources say California, New York and Massachusetts are not members, and Louisiana advanced a bill in March 2026. The **Counseling Compact** (LPCs) is still rolling out, with live-state counts that differ between sources. — [ASPR TRACIE: PSYPACT](https://asprtracie.hhs.gov/technical-resources/resource/13518/psypact); [Triad (2026)](https://www.triadhq.com/blog/telehealth-across-state-lines-2026); [Triad compacts 2026](https://b2b.triadhq.com/blog/behavioral-health-licensure-compacts-2026); [Louisiana PSYPACT bill](https://cpo.soar.com/articles/7757473/Louisiana/House-panel-advances-PSYPACT-compact-to-expand-interstate-telepsychology) (vendor and press sources; verify at psypact.org and counselingcompact.org)
- Medical (not mental health) analog: a KeyCare survey of 400 commercially insured telehealth users found **68%** of those who needed care while traveling used telehealth. — [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/?p=217521)
- Alma survey of therapists: 67% have pursued or are considering licenses in additional states, and about half of them want to keep serving clients who moved across state lines. The methodology is unverified. — [ConnectWithCare / Alma](https://connectwithcare.org/?p=7074)

**College students moving between states [regulatory / reports]**
- Campus counselors generally cannot treat students who go home to another state over breaks. APA's college telehealth guidance lists cross-state practice as a barrier. One University of Iowa example says more than 20% of students live in Illinois and cannot be treated by the counseling center when home (as surfaced in coverage; I could not verify the specific article). Some schools contract with national telehealth vendors (for example NC State with AcademicLiveCare, "providers in all 50 states," covering winter break). Others, such as NEOMED, require students to be physically in Ohio. — [APA college telehealth best practices](https://www.psychiatry.org/getattachment/6e0398be-9120-4e2f-b826-e64f37112b0c/Resource-Document-College-Telehealth-Best-Practices.pdf); [Higher Ed Dive](https://www.highereddive.com/news/colleges-use-teletherapy-to-support-students-outside-of-the-academic-year/625532/); [NC State](https://news.dasa.ncsu.edu/nc-state-announces-partnership-with-academiclivecare); [NEOMED](https://thepulse.neomed.edu/news/we-are-here-to-support-you/); [WFSB (2022)](https://www.wfsb.com/2022/09/26/mental-health-options-college-students-attending-schools-other-states-can-be-challenge)
- 29.8% of adolescents with MDE who got treatment received it at a **school** counseling center, so school-based care is a major channel that breaks around holidays and summer. — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm)

### Inferences
- **Rough sizing, an inference rather than a sourced figure:** roughly 32M adults received any mental health treatment in 2024 (NSDUH), and about 14% of adults received counseling (NHIS), which is on the order of 35M people. Only people already in ongoing therapy can have a "my therapist is unavailable" or "I'm traveling" gap. Most of those gaps are short (a week of vacation) and clinically low-stakes for stable clients, and many therapists cover them with a colleague or telehealth. The "traveling or therapist unavailable today" scenario is therefore probably a **niche, low-frequency, low-willingness-to-pay** need compared with (a) bridging waitlists of 1–3 months, (b) "one session for one issue" demand and (c) after-hours or short-notice support, where the competition is AI chatbots and 988.
- **Disconfirming points for the original angle:**
  - Licensure limits a cross-state one-off with a new clinician just as much as continuity with the client's own therapist. Compacts help both equally, so a stranger has no structural advantage.
  - A one-off with a stranger lacks the alliance that predicts retention.
  - Employer platforms (Spring Health) and national vendors (AcademicLiveCare) already serve the traveling and college-break cases.
  - "No openings" is cited by only 16.5% of adults with unmet need, against 65% citing cost and 45–49% citing navigation.
- The **strongest gap scenarios** for a one-off product, ranked by evidence: (1) **waitlist bridging** and **between-therapist** periods after discharge or retirement; (2) **single-issue, single-session** consultations, which have a clinical evidence base and a modal-session reality; (3) **college students at home over breaks** (structural licensure gap, concentrated seasonally); (4) **therapist on extended leave**, where clients may be discharged; (5) travel, which is the weakest.

### Gaps
- **No data found** on how often clients travel during ongoing therapy, how often sessions are missed because a therapist is unavailable, or how many clients are between therapists after one retires or leaves.
- No US data on the number of college students who cross state lines for school **and** are in ongoing therapy.
- No data on willingness to pay for a one-off session with an unfamiliar therapist.

## 6. Underserved groups: rural, low-income/Medicaid, BIPOC, LGBTQ+, Deaf/HoH (ASL), non-English speakers, people with disabilities

### Takeaway
Access gaps are deepest where supply is thin and listings are least reliable. Those groups are Medicaid enrollees (about 18% of listed prescribers actually accessible), rural residents (about 40% of Americans live in a mental-health shortage area), LGBTQ+ youth (half who wanted care couldn't get it), Spanish speakers (about 5.5% of psychologists could serve them in Spanish, 2015 data) and Deaf ASL users (a handful of ASL-fluent clinicians per state). These are matching problems that telehealth plus specialized filters can partly solve within licensure limits.

### Cited Findings
- **Rural and shortage areas [Stat]:** as of Dec 2, 2025, about **137M people (40% of the US)** lived in a Mental Health Professional Shortage Area. — [HRSA Behavioral Health Workforce Brief 2025](https://bhw.hrsa.gov/sites/default/files/bureau-health-workforce/data-research/Behavioral-Health-Workforce-Brief-2025.pdf) (figure via search summary). Geographic mental-health HPSAs numbered 6,807 at the end of 2025, up from 6,418. — [Missouri Rural Health Info Center](https://ruralhealthinfocenter.health.mo.gov/2025-national-shortage-designation-update/) (secondary). MHA reports that the South and more rural states face the largest access gaps. — [Healthline on MHA 2025](https://www.healthline.com/health-news/best-worst-states-mental-health-america)
- **Medicaid / low-income [Stat]:** 17.8% of listed Medicaid managed-care psychiatric prescribers were accessible (JAMA 2024), and Oregon Medicaid had 59–67% phantom mental health listings (Health Affairs 2022). — [JAMA](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2821639); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/study-finds-rampant-phantom-docs-medicaid-network-directories-oregon). Uninsured adults were about twice as likely as insured adults to use AI chatbots for mental health information (30% vs. 14%). — [ACMHAI summary of KFF poll (Mar 2026)](https://acmhai.org/news/kff-tracking-poll-on-health-information-and-trust-use-of-ai-for-health-information-and-advice/)
- **BIPOC and working-class [Stat]:** working-class Black callers received therapist callbacks 34% of the time, against 51% for middle-class white callers, and appointment offers about 8% of the time against 28% (2016 NYC audit). — [ASA](https://www.asanet.org/news_item/study-finds-evidence-racial-and-class-discrimination-among-psychotherapists/)
- **LGBTQ+ youth [Stat]:** in the Trevor Project's 2024 US survey, **50%** of LGBTQ+ young people who wanted mental health care in the past year couldn't get it. Reasons included fear of talking about mental health, cost, not wanting to get a parent's permission and fear of being outed. Access was highest in the Northeast (56%) and lowest in the South (45%). — [Trevor Project 2024 survey](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/survey-2024/); [Trevor research brief (Sept 2024)](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Mental-Health-Care-Access-and-Use-among-LGBTQ-Young-People.pdf). 988's LGBTQ+ youth "Press 3" option was **discontinued July 17, 2025**, after reaching 69,571 monthly contacts in June 2025. — [VoxBooster blog citing GAO](https://voxbooster.com/blog/988-crisis-lifeline-call-volume-statistics-2026) (secondary)
- **Non-English speakers [Stat]:** in APA's 2015 survey, only **5.5% of psychologists** could provide services in Spanish. — [APA Monitor (2018)](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2018/06/spanish-speaking). In Texas, fewer than 20% of 10,440 responding mental health providers offered services in any language other than English (2023 workforce survey). — [Texas Tribune (2024)](https://www.texastribune.org/2024/04/19/texas-mental-health-providers-diversity)
- **Deaf / hard of hearing [Stat/reports]:** the National Association of the Deaf describes a "nationwide acute shortage" of ASL-fluent mental health clinicians and endorses videoconferencing as a partial remedy. — [NAD position statement](https://www.nad.org/?p=2647). State snapshots: Nebraska listed only **8 known ASL-fluent mental health providers** (older page), Wisconsin reported **2** licensed counselors who use ASL as a first language, and Minnesota (2016) reported waitlists of months for ASL-fluent therapists. — [Nebraska Commission for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing](https://ncdhh.nebraska.gov/node/23); [TMJ4](https://www.tmj4.com/news/local-news/urgent-need-for-mental-health-resources-for-deaf-hard-of-hearing-community); [MinnPost (2016)](https://www.minnpost.com/mental-health-addiction/2016/01/alison-aubrecht-peer-support-program-takes-deaf-centric-approach-men/)
- **Youth (privacy and stigma) [Stat]:** among adolescents with MDE and unmet need, 70.5% worried what people would think and 64.9% worried about privacy. — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm)

### Inferences
- For scarce-specialty groups (ASL, Spanish and other languages, LGBTQ-affirming care), the bottleneck is **discoverability across state lines**: the few qualified clinicians are spread nationally. A matching product with verified specialty and language filters, combined with compact-enabled telehealth, gives these groups much more value per match than it gives the median user. This is also where a one-off "consult with a specialist who shares my identity or language" could be most valued.
- Medicaid has the worst directory accuracy but low per-session revenue. Serving it probably requires a B2B or plan partnership (network-adequacy compliance) rather than consumer pay.

### Gaps
- **Disabilities and app accessibility:** I found no data on accessibility of therapy apps or directories (WCAG compliance, captioning, screen-reader support), and none on unmet need among people with disabilities. This needs dedicated research.
- No current national count of ASL-fluent or non-English-speaking clinicians. The Spanish figure dates from 2015.
- No 2025–2026 data on BIPOC-specific unmet need or race-concordant matching demand beyond the 2016 audit.

## 7. What fills the gap today: 988, warmlines, peer support, AI chatbots, urgent care/ER. What works and what doesn't?

### Takeaway
When people can't get a session, they increasingly turn to **AI chatbots**. In March 2026, 16% of US adults had used one for mental health information in the past year, rising to 28% of under-30s and 30% of the uninsured, and 58% of those users did not follow up with a provider. Users cite **immediacy** above all. **988** is growing (about 19M contacts through Sept 2025) and is now widely known, but it is crisis-oriented. **Peer platforms** like 7 Cups are free and always on but carry documented safety and quality concerns. **ERs** are a costly last resort, with boarding in 1 in 10 youth Medicaid mental-health visits. I found no evaluative data on warmlines.

### Cited Findings
**AI chatbots [Stat]**
- KFF Tracking Poll (Feb 24–Mar 2, 2026; n=1,343): **16% of adults** used AI chatbots for mental health information in the past year, against 29% for physical health. The rate was **28% for adults under 30 vs. 8% for those 50+**, and **30% for the uninsured vs. 14% for the insured**. About 65% cited quick or immediate information or support as a major reason. **58% of mental-health users did not follow up with a doctor or provider.** — [KFF news release](https://www.kff.org/health-information-trust/poll-1-in-3-adults-are-turning-to-ai-chatbots-for-health-information-equaling-the-share-who-use-social-media-for-health/); [KFF poll finding](https://www.kff.org/public-opinion/kff-tracking-poll-on-health-information-and-trust-use-of-ai-for-health-information-and-advice/); [Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/32-of-adults-turn-to-ai-for-health-advice-kff/); [ACMHAI](https://acmhai.org/news/kff-tracking-poll-on-health-information-and-trust-use-of-ai-for-health-information-and-advice/)
- Syracuse Lerner Center brief (Dec 2025; adults 18–49): nearly **35% use AI tools more than once a week** to manage mental health concerns. A search summary also reported that 28.4% of those who had seen a human professional now see one less often. I could not verify that figure, and the methodology is not shown on the page. — [Lerner Center](https://www.maxwell.syr.edu/research/lerner-center/population-health-research-brief-series/article/is-ai-replacing-human-mental-health-professionals)
- Youth aged 12–21: 19.2% used AI chatbots for mental health advice in 2025, up from about 13% a year earlier, and 63.3% had not told anyone. — [ConsumerAffairs](https://consumeraffairs.com/news/more-young-people-are-turning-to-ai-chatbots-for-mental-health-advice-study-finds-060326.html); [Bioethics.com (JAMA Pediatrics study)](https://bioethics.com/archives/102906) (secondary)
- Verasight (Sept 2025, n=353 AI-for-mental-health users, ±6.8%): 74% said they would still have someone else to talk to if they lost chatbot access. That suggests chatbots supplement human support more often than they replace it. — [Verasight](https://data.verasight.io/ai/most-americans-have-someone-besides-an-ai-chatbot-to-talk-to-about-mental-health)
- Safety signal (UK, non-US): in a Mental Health UK poll, 37% of adults had used AI chatbots for mental health support, and 11% of users said it triggered or worsened symptoms of psychosis. — [Healthcare Management UK](https://www.healthcare-management.uk/ai-chatbots-mental-health-support)

**988 Lifeline [Stat]**
- GAO-26-108114: about **19.1M** calls, texts and chats were routed July 2022–Sept 2025. Over that period calls rose about 87%, texts about 260% and chats about 23%. Call answer rates improved, while text and chat answer rates fluctuated. Calls are now georouted to approximate location, and texts are to follow by April 2027. SAMHSA's goal is for local centers to answer more than 90% of texts and chats by Sept 2026. — [GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-108114). Secondary: 23.3M contacts through March 2026, with March 2026 volume 15% above a year earlier. — [VoxBooster citing KFF](https://voxbooster.com/blog/988-crisis-lifeline-call-volume-statistics-2026) (unverified)
- Awareness (NAMI/Ipsos, Summer 2025): about 70% had at least heard of 988 (82% of ages 18–29), but only **28% were familiar with it**, double the 14% of Sept 2022. — [NAMI 2025 poll](https://www.nami.org/support-education/publications-reports/survey-reports/poll-of-public-perspectives-on-988-crisis-response-2025/). In 2022, more than half knew nothing about 988. — [CNN/KFF](https://amp.cnn.com/cnn/2022/10/08/politics/cnn-kff-mental-health-survey-what-matters/index.html)
- Limits: 988 is a crisis line, not ongoing or one-off therapy, and the LGBTQ+ youth specialized line was cut in July 2025 (see Section 6).

**Peer support / 7 Cups [Reports / Anecdotal]**
- 7 Cups offers free 24/7 chat with volunteer "listeners" and says it is **not a crisis service**. Reporting (Everyday Health 2024, Mashable) describes very brief listener training, concerns from current and former staff about the safety of minors, trolling and harassment by listeners, and requests for personal information. The company disputes the most serious claims and cites a trust-score system. — [Wikiwand: 7 Cups](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/7_Cups); [Former listener account (Medium)](https://medium.com/@julliardlin/my-experience-as-a-7-cups-volunteer-listener-ab3f68fd0c3e); [Healthline](https://www.healthline.com/health/mental-health/7-cups)
- Adults with AMI: 2024 NSDUH lists ER use for mental health treatment at 3.7% (7.6% for SMI). Among adolescents with MDE, 14.9% used support groups and 9.1% used peer support specialists. — [2024 NSDUH detailed tables](https://www.samhsa.gov/data/sites/default/files/reports/rpt56287/2024-nsduh-annual-national/2024-nsduh-annual-national-html-071425-edited/2024-nsduh-annual-national.htm)

**ER / boarding [Stat]**
- JAMA Health Forum (2025; 2022 Medicaid claims): **more than 1 in 10** youth mental-health ED visits ended in boarding, ranging from 2.7% (Arkansas) to 27.3% (Iowa). — [JAMA Health Forum](https://media.jamanetwork.com/10.1001/jamahealthforum.2025.3177); [Drugs.com/HealthDay](https://www.drugs.com/news/one-10-mental-health-related-ed-visits-among-youths-involve-boarding-126336.html). In Massachusetts, **37.5%** of mental-health ED patients were boarded in 2024. — [Axios Boston (Apr 2026)](https://www.axios.com/local/boston/2026/04/02/massachusetts-mental-health-emergency-boarding)

### Inferences
- The fastest-growing gap-filler, AI chatbots, wins on **immediacy, zero cost and privacy**. Those are the same dimensions where human therapy access fails: the top NSDUH reasons are cost, not knowing where to go, time, and stigma or privacy for youth. A human one-off-session product competes directly with free AI for low-acuity, in-the-moment needs. Its differentiation would have to be **human judgment, safety and handoff to ongoing care**, ideally available same-day.
- 58% of AI users don't follow up with a provider. That points to a possible **"AI-to-human escalation" funnel**, where a chatbot or triage layer routes users to a same-day human session or a verified match. This is a plausible partnership or positioning wedge (inference).
- 988 and ERs serve crisis. Peer lines serve listening but carry safety risk. That leaves a **sub-crisis, same-day, professional** tier ("I need to talk to a clinician today, but I'm not in crisis") that is thinly served. This, rather than travel, is the clearest white space a one-off-session model could own.

### Gaps
- **Warmlines:** I found no outcome or usage data for US warmlines (volume, hours, satisfaction, effect on 988 or ER use).
- No US data on urgent-care (non-ER) use for mental health.
- No data on how often people use AI chatbots specifically **because** they couldn't get a session or their therapist was unavailable. Current polls measure general use, not substitution in specific gaps.
