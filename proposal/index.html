Design, Build, Ship · Project Proposal

Runway

A cash forecasting and liquidity dashboard that shows people with steady or irregular income where their money will be over the next week to the next year. It also helps them plan big decisions without hiring a financial advisor.

The problem

Budget apps look backward. They tell you whether you met last month's budget, as a yes or a no. They don't tell you whether you'll be short in three weeks, how much cash you can safely invest, or whether you can afford a big purchase in March. Companies and funds answer those questions with treasury tools: rolling cash forecasts, liquidity tiers and stress tests. Individuals mostly get them through a financial advisor, or through platforms like Fidelity and Northwestern Mutual that assume a steady paycheck.

Who it's for

Anyone who wants to know where their money is going next, whatever their income looks like. Runway is built for the whole range:

Irregular income Steady income

Everyone on that range needs the same thing: one forward-looking view of cash across every account, built from their income streams, current assets and goals. The app adapts to the person rather than assuming a steady paycheck. Lumpy income widens the forecast range; steady income narrows it.

Accessibility is part of the point

  • No advisor required. The planning tools wealthier clients get from an advisor or a firm like Fidelity, available to anyone.
  • Free to use. No account minimums and no assets-under-management fees.
  • Plain language. Every forecast and suggestion explains itself in everyday words, with no finance jargon required.
  • Usable by everyone. Readable on a phone, works with a keyboard and screen reader, and charts that don't rely on color alone.

I interviewed a freelancer and two MBA students.

MBA student

Their answers match what I found in research. Apps that do forecast (PocketSmith, Simplifi, Monarch) project recurring bills as a single line. Cash sweeps (Wealthfront, Betterment) are fixed rules inside one provider. Stress tests exist only in retirement planners. Nothing combines them across linked accounts.

What it does

  1. Link accounts. Connect several bank and brokerage accounts into one view.
  2. Describe your income streams. Salary, freelance clients, bonuses or stipends, each with its own timing and confidence. An irregular stream widens the forecast range; a steady one narrows it.
  3. Add goals and planned uses of cash. Tuition, a trip, a down payment, a planned investment contribution. Each is entered by hand with a date and amount.
  4. See the dashboard. Projected cash at T+1 week, T+2 weeks, T+1 month, T+3 months, T+6 months and T+1 year, each shown as a low/base/high range. You choose how wide or cautious the range is. Shortfalls are flagged with the reason.
  5. Position cash. Set targets for operating cash (checking), reserve (high-yield savings) and long-term money (investments). The app suggests moves such as "move $2,000 from checking to savings; nothing large is due for 3 weeks." You make the transfers yourself.
  6. Stress test. Run "lose my biggest client," "$1,500 car repair," "bonus is half" or "market −30%" and see how many weeks of runway you have left.
  7. Ask. "Can I afford a $3,000 trip in March? Should I?" An AI assistant answers by running the forecast and scenarios. The numbers come from the engine, and the model explains them.

Behind the scenes, I'll track forecast vs. actual accuracy as a designer's metric to tune the model. Clients won't see it.

Focus: Holistic Journey

The value only shows up when the whole path works end to end. A person has to be able to sign up, link accounts, describe their income and goals, see a forecast they believe, and act on it. A beautiful dashboard with fake data, or a clever model nobody can sign up for, would miss the point. I'd rather every step work on the live site with moderate depth than polish one piece in isolation. The AI assistant is a v2 add-on, not the core.

  • Real accounts through Teller (dev)
  • Internal forecast-accuracy tracking

Leaving out

  • Moving money for users. That's money transmission, which needs licenses or a partner bank. Runway recommends; you act.
  • Advice on specific securities. That would make it an investment adviser. Stress tests use broad market scenarios, not picks.
  • Bill pay, a native mobile app and tax planning.

The most ambitious version

A personal treasury desk. It learns your real income patterns, keeps a forecast that improves with time across every account you own, and tells you each week what to do with your cash. It runs the same stress tests a fund would run on its own book.

Tech stack

PieceChoice and why
AppNext.js on Vercel. Pages and server routes in one project. Bank API secrets stay in server routes, never in the browser.
Data and authSupabase (Postgres, Auth, row-level security). Financial data needs strict per-user access, and Postgres suits time-series transactions and forecasts.
Bank dataPlaid Sandbox for v1: free fake banks, plus built-in recurring-transaction detection. Teller (development) for v2: up to 100 real bank logins, free. Both sit behind one shared interface, so either can be swapped.
Market and rate dataFRED API (free) for interest rates and inflation in stress tests. Alpha Vantage (free, cached nightly) for broad market moves.
Forecast enginePlain TypeScript: recurring flows plus manual streams and goals, with ranges from historical variance. It's deterministic so the numbers are explainable and testable.
AIClaude API with tool use. The model calls forecast() and run_scenario() rather than doing math itself. Evals compare its answers to the engine's output.
ChartsChart.js: lightweight, accessible range bands.

MCP servers: the Supabase MCP server for schema and migrations (read-only on any real data), and Plaid's sandbox MCP server to generate test users and transactions.

CLIs: Claude Code, the Vercel CLI (deploys, vercel env pull), the Supabase CLI (migrations) and the GitHub CLI (gh).

Everything stays on free tiers: Vercel Hobby, Supabase Free, Plaid Sandbox, Teller dev, FRED and Alpha Vantage. The Claude API key will have a hard spending cap and usage alerts.
